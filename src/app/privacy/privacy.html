Code à Cuisine is a private learning and portfolio project. This page explains in plain words which data is used, what for and where it goes.

In short

  • You do not need an account, and you never enter your name or your e-mail address.
  • There are no cookies, no tracking and no analytics.
  • Your ingredients and preferences are used to create recipes, and the recipes are public in the cookbook.

Hosting of the website

The website is hosted by IONOS SE, Germany. When you open a page, the server stores technical data in log files, for example your IP address, the time, the page and your browser. This is needed to deliver the website safely and is done by every web server.

Creating recipes

When you create recipes, your ingredients, the number of portions and cooks, the cooking time, the cuisine and the diet are sent to an automation server (n8n) on a virtual server of IONOS SE, Germany. The server also sees your IP address, to protect the service against too many requests. From there, the recipe request is passed on to the AI service myAI of Swisscom (Schweiz) AG, Switzerland. Your IP address is not passed on to the AI service.

While you type an ingredient, the first letters are sent through the same server to Open Food Facts, a free food database, to show suggestions.

The public cookbook

Every created recipe and the hearts it gets are saved in Cloud Firestore, a database of Google (Google Ireland Limited and Google LLC, USA). Your browser connects to Google directly to load and save recipes, so Google sees your IP address. Data can be processed in the USA. The recipes stay in the cookbook without a time limit and everybody can read them. Please do not enter personal data as an ingredient.

Stored in your browser

The website remembers in the storage of your browser which recipes you gave a heart and whether you have already seen the "Better Life" hint. While the tab is open, it also keeps your current ingredients and your last results. This data never leaves your device, and you can delete it in the settings of your browser at any time.

Your rights

You can ask which data about you is processed, and you can ask to correct or delete it. If a recipe in the cookbook should be removed, write an e-mail to {{ contactEmail }}. You can also complain to the Swiss data protection authority (FDPIC) or to a data protection authority in your country.
